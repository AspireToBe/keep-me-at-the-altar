Before you begin

Fasting is not a discipline.
It is an encounter.

Before you fast, you must understand what you are doing and why. This is not religion. This is one of the most ancient and powerful spiritual weapons ever entrusted to God's people.

"Is not this the kind of fasting I have chosen: to loose the chains of injustice… to share your food with the hungry? Then your light will break forth like the dawn." — Isaiah 58:6–8

Section 1 · Foundation

What fasting really is —
and what it is not.

At its core, biblical fasting is not about food. It is a spiritual discipline — a deliberate, temporary denial of something physical in order to intensify prayer, sharpen spiritual clarity, and deepen encounter with God.

Fasting is not a diet. It is not a way to earn God's favour, impress others, or control His will. It is an act of humble worship — a declaration that you are hungry for God above all else. Jesus did not say if you fast — He said when (Matthew 6:16–17).

"Fasting is not the absence of food. It is the presence of God."
??
Fasting Humbles
It strips away comfort and self-reliance, reminding us of our complete dependence on God. Hunger is a physical prayer.
??
Fasting Focuses
It removes distractions and sharpens spiritual discernment. God doesn't speak louder — you go quieter.
⛓
Fasting Releases
Throughout Scripture, fasting accompanies repentance, dependence, and seeking God. Some things only come out by prayer and fasting (Matthew 17:21).

Section 2 · Biblical evidence

They fasted.
God answered.

From Genesis to Revelation, God's people have used fasting as a weapon, a posture, and a pathway to encounter. These are not obscure stories — they are the backbone of Scripture.

Moses
Exodus 34:28 · Deuteronomy 9:9
Moses fasted 40 days and nights — twice — while receiving the Law on Mount Sinai. No food, no water. Sustained entirely by God's presence.
What followed: The Ten Commandments. The covenant between God and Israel.
Esther
Esther 4:16
Facing the genocide of her people, Esther called a three-day absolute fast before approaching the king uninvited — at the cost of her life.
What followed: The king extended his sceptre. An entire people was saved.
Daniel
Daniel 1:8–17 · Daniel 10:2–3
Daniel fasted from the king's food and received wisdom beyond his peers. He fasted 21 days for revelation — an angel appeared on Day 1 but was delayed by spiritual warfare.
What followed: Supernatural revelation, angelic visitation, prophetic understanding.
Nehemiah
Nehemiah 1:4
When Nehemiah heard Jerusalem's walls were broken, he fasted and prayed for days before making a single move or presenting a single request.
What followed: The king's favour. Resources. The walls rebuilt in 52 days.
Jehoshaphat
2 Chronicles 20:3–4
When three armies marched against Judah simultaneously, King Jehoshaphat proclaimed a national fast. All of Judah came together to seek God.
What followed: God set the enemy armies against each other. Judah never had to fight.
Anna the Prophetess
Luke 2:36–38
A widow who never left the temple, fasting and praying night and day for decades. She was 84 years old — still at the altar.
What followed: She was present the day Jesus was brought to the temple and declared His identity.
Jesus
Matthew 4:1–11
Before performing a single miracle or preaching a single sermon, Jesus fasted 40 days in the wilderness. Tempted at His weakest — and overcame.
What followed: He returned in the power of the Spirit. After the wilderness, Jesus began His public ministry in the power of the Spirit.
Paul
Acts 9:9 · 2 Corinthians 11:27
After his encounter with the risen Christ, Paul fasted three days — blind, without food or water. He later described fasting often as part of his normal apostolic life.
What followed: His sight was restored, his calling commissioned, his ministry launched.
The Early Church
Acts 13:2–3 · Acts 14:23
Before commissioning Paul and Barnabas, the church at Antioch fasted and prayed. Before appointing elders, they fasted and prayed. Major moments of seeking God in Scripture were often accompanied by fasting.
What followed: The gospel spread across the known world.
"In every case, the fast was not the end — it was the beginning. It opened the door to what God was about to do."

Section 3 · Types of fasting

Not all fasts are the same.
Match the fast to the moment.

Scripture and Christian tradition offer a range of fasting approaches. Choosing the right type is not a legalistic exercise — it is a prayerful act of wisdom.

01
Absolute Fast
No food · No water · 1–3 days max
+

No food or water. Reserved for extreme spiritual urgency — crisis, life-or-death intercession. Never routine.

"Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day." — Esther 4:16
Medical caution: Always consult a physician. Do not exceed 3 days. Requires pastoral covering.
02
Normal / Total Fast
Water only · 1, 3, 7, or 21 days
+

Water only — no food. The most common biblical fast. Focuses mind and body entirely on spiritual seeking. Jesus fasted this way 40 days.

"After fasting forty days and forty nights, he was hungry." — Matthew 4:2
03
Daniel Fast
Vegetables, fruit, water, wholegrains · 7–21 days
+

No meat, no sweets, no processed food. Drawn from Daniel's practice in Babylon. The most accessible form of extended fasting — sustainable over weeks.

"Give us nothing but vegetables to eat and water to drink." — Daniel 1:12
04
Intermittent / Partial Fast
Eating within set windows · Daily practice
+

Eating only during specific windows — e.g. breaking fast at 3pm (the biblical hour of prayer). Excellent for daily fasting practice and busy seasons.

"Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour." — Acts 3:1
05
Media / Sensory Fast
No screens · No entertainment · No noise
+

Abstaining from social media, television, news, entertainment, or digital noise. Reclaims mental space for God's voice. Many find this harder than the food fast — which reveals exactly why it is necessary.

"Be still, and know that I am God." — Psalm 46:10
06
Corporate / Congregational Fast
Fasting in community · Multiplied authority
+

Fasting in unity with a church body or prayer group. Corporate fasting positions us in dependence before God. If you can find even one person to fast with, do it.

"Blow the trumpet in Zion, declare a holy fast, call a sacred assembly." — Joel 2:15

Practical guidance

The Fasting Guide

Everything you need to fast well — spiritually and physically. Return to this page any time during your fast.

During your fast

What to do

Fasting is not just about what you stop. It is about what you start — intentional pursuit of God in the space created by the fast.

🙏 Pray with intention

Use your hunger as a prayer trigger. Every time you feel the urge to eat, turn it into a moment of prayer. Pray at the four daily prayer times — 9am, 12pm, 3pm, and 6pm.

📖 Read Scripture

Let fasting sharpen your focus on the Word. Read slowly. Meditate on one passage at a time. Let God speak through what you read.

✍️ Journal what God says

Write everything — impressions, scriptures, dreams, words. Fasting opens your spiritual sensitivity. Don't let what God says go unrecorded.

🤫 Practise silence

Set aside at least 15 minutes each day to sit in complete silence before God. No phone, no music, no agenda. Just you and Him.

🙌 Worship

Play worship music. Sing. Lift your hands. Worship shifts the atmosphere around you and within you, especially during a fast.

💧 Stay hydrated

Drink plenty of water throughout the day — especially on a full or partial fast. Herbal teas (unsweetened) are also fine on most fasts.

During your fast

What to avoid

Fasting without discipline in other areas can undermine the spiritual work. Guard the fast in every dimension.

📱 Social media & entertainment

A fast from food while feasting on content defeats the purpose. Reduce screen time significantly. Use the time you'd spend scrolling for prayer and Scripture instead.

💬 Negative conversation

Gossip, complaining, and conflict are especially harmful during a fast. Isaiah 58 is clear — the fast God chooses involves right relationships, not just right eating.

😤 Striving and overworking

Fasting is not a performance. It is surrender. Resist the temptation to use fasting as leverage — as if you are making God owe you something.

🍫 Cheat eating

If you are on a Daniel Fast or partial fast, be honest with yourself. Sneaking food you have committed to give up breaks the covenant of the fast, not just the diet.

😴 Excessive sleep

Use the extra mental space fasting creates for God, not for extra hours in bed. Early mornings during a fast are some of the most powerful prayer times.

⚡ Overexertion

Especially on a total or extended fast, do not push your body to its limits physically. Rest is appropriate. Listen to your body.

Ending your fast

How to break a fast

Breaking a fast is as important as the fast itself — spiritually and physically. Do not rush it.

Spiritually

Before you eat anything, take a moment to close the fast in prayer. Thank God for what He has done and said during the fast. Declare what you are carrying forward. Do not let the fast end without a moment of worship.

"The fast does not end when you eat — it ends when you have sealed what God has done in you."

Physically — breaking it gently

After a 1-day fast

Start with water, fruit juice, or a small piece of fruit. Wait 30 minutes before eating a light meal such as soup, salad, or steamed vegetables.

After a 3–7 day fast

Reintroduce food gradually over 1–2 days. Begin with diluted juices and broths, then soft fruits and vegetables, then normal foods. Avoid heavy, fried, or processed food immediately.

After a 14–21 day fast

Treat the re-feeding period with great care. Spend the same number of days reintroducing food as you spent fasting (up to a week). Start with juices, then broths, then soft foods. Listen to your body closely.

On a Daniel Fast

Transition back to normal food gradually. Introduce animal proteins and dairy slowly — your digestive system will have adjusted to plant-based foods.

Physical experience

What you might feel — and what it means

Your body will respond to fasting. Most of what you feel is normal. Understanding it keeps you from giving up prematurely.

Headaches

Normal

Usually caused by caffeine withdrawal or dehydration. Drink more water. If you normally drink coffee, reduce it gradually before a fast rather than stopping abruptly.

Dizziness

Normal (mild)

Especially when standing up quickly. Move slowly, rest when needed. If severe, break the fast and consult a doctor.

Fatigue & weakness

Normal

Your body is conserving energy. This is normal. Rest when needed and drink plenty of water.

Hunger pangs

Normal

They come in waves and usually pass within 20 minutes. Use them as prayer prompts. The hunger itself is part of the discipline.

Heightened sensitivity

Expected

Dreams, impressions, clarity of thought, and a deeper awareness of God's presence are common during extended fasting. Take note of everything.

Irritability

Normal

Fasting can surface things buried under comfort and routine. This is not a sign to stop — it is often a sign that God is dealing with something deeper.

Bad breath

Normal

A sign your body is in ketosis (burning fat for energy). Stay hydrated and brush regularly.

Nausea

Sometimes

Often occurs in the first day or two. Rest, sip water slowly. If persistent or severe, break the fast gently.

⚠️ Stop and seek medical advice if you experience:

Fainting or loss of consciousness · Severe dizziness or confusion · Chest pain or difficulty breathing · Persistent vomiting · Severe or unusual weakness · Any symptom that concerns you

Do not continue a fast through serious symptoms. Breaking a fast early is wisdom, not failure.

Important

When not to fast from food

God honours your desire to fast. He does not require you to harm your body to do so. If any of the following apply, consider a non-food fast instead — a media fast, a social media fast, or a fast from entertainment.

  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Diabetes or blood sugar conditions
  • Eating disorders (past or present)
  • Heart conditions or serious illness
  • Medication that requires food
  • Children under 18 — consult a parent or guardian
  • Post-surgery recovery

Always consult your doctor before beginning an extended fast, especially if you have any underlying health conditions. Keep Me At The Altar is a spiritual platform, not a medical one. Your health matters to God.
